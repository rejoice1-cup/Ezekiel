1. HOW TO STOP INFECTION

Note: This is general health information only. For diagnosis and treatment, please speak with a doctor.

An "infection" happens when germs like bacteria, viruses, fungi, or parasites get into your body and multiply. The goal of stopping infection is 2 things: prevent it from starting + stop it from spreading/worsening once it starts.

A. HOW TO PREVENT INFECTION BEFORE IT STARTS

Prevention is the best way. Think of it as building a wall.

1. Personal Hygiene

  • Handwashing: Wash with soap and water for 20 seconds. Key times: after toilet, before eating, after being outside, after touching animals. If no water, use alcohol-based sanitizer with at least 60% alcohol.
  • Bathing: Daily bath helps remove germs from skin. Pay attention to folds: armpits, groin, between toes.
  • Oral hygiene: Brush 2x daily. Mouth infections can spread to the body.

2. Food and Water Safety

  • Wash fruits and vegetables well.
  • Cook meat, eggs, and seafood thoroughly. "Well done" kills bacteria like salmonella and E.coli.
  • Drink clean water. Boil or use treated water if unsure.
  • Avoid food left out for >2 hours.

3. Wound Care

Any cut is an open door for germs.

  • Clean immediately with clean water and soap
  • Apply antiseptic like iodine or methylated spirit
  • Cover with clean bandage/plaster
  • Change dressing daily or if it gets wet/dirty
  • Watch for redness, swelling, pus, heat. That means infection is starting.

4. Safe Practices

  • Safe sex: Use condoms to prevent STIs like HIV, gonorrhea, chlamydia
  • Safe injections: Never share needles. Only use new, sterile ones at hospitals/clinics
  • Vaccinations: Vaccines train your body to fight viruses before they infect you. Examples: Hepatitis B, Tetanus, COVID, HPV
  • Avoid sharing: Toothbrush, towels, razors, nail clippers can spread fungal and bacterial infections

5. Boost Your Body’s Defense - Immune System

  • Sleep: 7-8 hours. Sleep is when your body repairs
  • Nutrition: Eat protein, fruits, vegetables. Vitamin C, Zinc, Vitamin D help immunity
  • Water: 2-3 liters daily helps flush germs
  • Exercise: 30 mins 3-4x per week improves circulation of immune cells
  • Stress: Chronic stress weakens immunity

B. HOW TO STOP AN INFECTION ONCE IT HAS STARTED

If you already have symptoms, you need to act fast so it doesn’t spread.

1. Identify the type of infection

Different germs need different treatment. Don’t self-medicate blindly.

  • Bacterial: Often causes pus, fever, localized pain, swelling. Needs antibiotics prescribed by a doctor
  • Viral: Cough, cold, flu, body aches, fever. Antibiotics don’t work. Needs rest, fluids, sometimes antivirals
  • Fungal: Itching, white patches, athlete’s foot, thrush. Needs antifungals
  • Parasitic: Stomach issues, worms, malaria. Needs antiparasitics

2. General steps you can take at home for mild cases

  • Rest: Your body uses energy to fight
  • Hydration: Fever and diarrhea cause fluid loss. Take ORS, water, coconut water
  • Fever management: Paracetamol can help. Don’t use aspirin in children
  • Don’t pick/squeeze: Pimples, boils. This pushes infection deeper
  • Isolate if contagious: Cold, flu, COVID, chickenpox. Wear mask, avoid close contact

3. When to see a healthcare professional IMMEDIATELY

Go to a clinic or hospital if you notice:

  • Fever above 38.5°C that won’t go down
  • Pus, increasing redness, red streaks from a wound
  • Difficulty breathing, chest pain
  • Severe diarrhea or vomiting for >24 hours
  • Pain that is severe and not improving
  • Signs in babies, elderly, or people with diabetes/weak immunity

Doctors may do tests and give: antibiotics, antivirals, antifungals, or admit you for IV treatment.

4. Finish your treatment

If given antibiotics, finish the full course even if you feel better. Stopping early makes bacteria stronger and causes "antibiotic resistance".

C. STOPPING SPREAD IN COMMUNITY

  • Cover mouth when coughing/sneezing
  • Stay home when sick
  • Disinfect surfaces: door handles, phones with disinfectant
  • Proper waste disposal

Bottom line: Stop infection with hygiene + strong immunity. Treat early with the right medicine from a professional.

2. TYPES OF INFECTION IN THE BODY

Note: This is general health information only. For diagnosis and treatment, please speak with a doctor.

Infections are grouped by the type of germ that causes them and where they occur in the body.

A. BY THE TYPE OF GERM

1. Bacterial Infections

Caused by single-celled bacteria. Some bacteria are good, but bad ones make you sick.

Common examples:

  • Strep throat - sore throat, fever
  • UTI - burning during urination, frequent urination
  • Pneumonia - cough, chest pain, difficulty breathing
  • Food poisoning - Salmonella, E.coli - diarrhea, vomiting
  • Skin infections - boils, cellulitis, impetigo

Treatment: Antibiotics prescribed by a doctor. Must finish the course.

2. Viral Infections

Viruses are smaller than bacteria and need human cells to multiply. Antibiotics don’t work on them.

Common examples:

  • Respiratory: Common cold, Flu, COVID-19, RSV
  • Gastro: Rotavirus, Norovirus - vomiting and diarrhea
  • Other: Chickenpox, Measles, Hepatitis A/B/C, HIV, Dengue, Herpes

Treatment: Rest, fluids, fever medicine. Some have antivirals like for HIV, Hepatitis C, Flu.

3. Fungal Infections

Fungi are like yeast and mold. They thrive in warm, moist places.

Common examples:

  • Skin: Athlete’s foot, Ringworm, Jock itch - itching, round rash
  • Mouth: Oral thrush - white patches
  • Genital: Vaginal yeast infection - itching, discharge
  • Lungs: Aspergillosis - in people with weak immunity

Treatment: Antifungal creams, tablets, or IV for severe cases.

4. Parasitic Infections

Parasites are organisms that live on/in a host and take nutrients.

Common examples:

  • Protozoa: Malaria - from mosquito. Causes fever, chills. Giardia - diarrhea
  • Worms: Tapeworm, Roundworm, Hookworm - from contaminated food/soil. Causes stomach pain, weight loss
  • Ectoparasites: Lice, Scabies, Bedbugs - itching, skin irritation

Treatment: Antiparasitic drugs. Prevention is clean water, treated mosquito nets.

B. BY THE PART OF THE BODY AFFECTED

1. Respiratory Tract Infections: Affect nose, throat, lungs. Examples: Cold, Sinusitis, Bronchitis, Pneumonia, TB. Symptoms: Cough, sneezing, sore throat, shortness of breath.

2. Gastrointestinal Infections: Affect stomach and intestines. Examples: Gastroenteritis, Typhoid, Cholera, Food poisoning. Symptoms: Diarrhea, vomiting, stomach cramps, fever.

3. Urinary Tract Infections - UTIs: Affect bladder and kidneys. More common in women. Symptoms: Burning urination, cloudy urine, lower belly pain, fever if it reaches kidneys.

4. Skin and Soft Tissue Infections: Affect skin. Examples: Cellulitis, Abscess, Fungal infection, Scabies. Symptoms: Redness, swelling, heat, pus, itching.

5. Sexually Transmitted Infections - STIs: Spread through sexual contact. Examples: Gonorrhea, Chlamydia, Syphilis, HIV, HPV, Herpes. Symptoms: Discharge, sores, pain, but some have no symptoms.

6. Bloodstream Infections / Sepsis: When infection gets into the blood. This is an emergency. Causes: Untreated UTI, pneumonia, wound infection. Symptoms: High fever, fast heart rate, confusion, low BP.

7. Bone and Joint Infections: Examples: Osteomyelitis, Septic arthritis. Symptoms: Severe pain, swelling, fever, inability to use limb.

8. Eye Infections: Examples: Conjunctivitis "pink eye", Stye. Symptoms: Redness, discharge, itching, blurry vision.

C. BY SEVERITY

  • Acute: Comes on fast, lasts days to weeks. Example: Flu
  • Chronic: Lasts months to years. Example: Hepatitis B, HIV, TB
  • Latent: Germ hides in body and can reactivate. Example: Chickenpox → Shingles

Key idea: Location + germ type determines treatment. That’s why lab tests like blood culture, urine test, swab are important.

3. HOW TO COUNT MENSTRUAL CYCLE

Note: This is general health information only. For concerns about irregular cycles, please speak with a doctor.

Your menstrual cycle is the time from the first day of one period to the first day of the next period. Counting it helps you track ovulation, plan, or know if something is off.

A. THE BASIC ANATOMY

The average cycle is 28 days, but "normal" is 21-35 days for adults. Teenagers can be 21-45 days.

4 phases happen:

  1. Menstrual Phase: Days 1-5 - Bleeding. Day 1 = first day of full bleeding
  2. Follicular Phase: Days 1-13 - Egg matures in ovary
  3. Ovulation: Around Day 14 - Egg is released. This is most fertile day
  4. Luteal Phase: Days 15-28 - Body prepares for pregnancy. If no pregnancy, lining sheds

B. STEP BY STEP: HOW TO COUNT

Step 1: Mark Day 1

Day 1 is the first day you see real red blood. Spotting before doesn’t count. Example: If period starts July 5, then July 5 = Day 1.

Step 2: Count until next Day 1

Count every day until your next period starts. That last day before new bleeding = last day of cycle. Example: Period July 5 to August 1. July has 31 days. So July 5-31 = 27 days. + 1 day in August = 28 days. Cycle length = 28 days.

Step 3: Do this for 3-6 months

One month can vary. Track 3-6 cycles to get your average. Add all cycle lengths ÷ number of months = average cycle.

C. HOW TO PREDICT OVULATION

Formula: Next Period Date - 14 days = Approx Ovulation Day
Ovulation usually happens 14 days before your next period, not 14 days after last period.

Example with 28-day cycle: Period starts July 5. Next period approx August 2. August 2 - 14 days = July 19 = Ovulation day. Fertile window = 5 days before ovulation + ovulation day = July 14-19

If your cycle is 30 days: Ovulation ≈ Day 16
If your cycle is 24 days: Ovulation ≈ Day 10

D. METHODS TO TRACK ACCURATELY

1. Calendar Method

Use phone calendar or paper. Mark Day 1 each month. After 3 months you’ll see pattern.

2. Apps

Flo, Clue, Period Tracker. You input Day 1 and symptoms, it predicts.

3. Body Signs

  • Cervical mucus: Around ovulation it becomes clear, stretchy like "egg white"
  • Basal Body Temperature: Temp rises 0.3-0.5°C after ovulation. Take with thermometer every morning
  • Ovulation strips: Urine test that detects LH surge 24-36hrs before ovulation
  • Symptoms: Mild pelvic pain "mittelschmerz", breast tenderness

E. WHAT IS CONSIDERED NORMAL VS NOT

Normal:

  • Cycle length 21-35 days
  • Period lasts 2-7 days
  • Bleeding: Soaking 1 pad/tampon every 3-4 hours is heavy

See a healthcare professional if:

  • Cycles <21 days or >35 days consistently
  • Periods stop for 3+ months and you’re not pregnant
  • Bleeding between periods
  • Soaking >1 pad per hour for several hours
  • Severe pain that stops you from daily activities
  • Trying to conceive for 12 months with no success

F. FACTORS THAT AFFECT YOUR CYCLE

Stress, sudden weight loss/gain, travel, illness, PCOS, thyroid issues, breastfeeding, birth control.

Tracking tips for The Hustle Table ladies:
  1. Pick one method and be consistent
  2. Track for at least 3 months before making decisions
  3. Note flow, pain, mood too - not just dates

Bottom line: Count from Day 1 to Day 1. Average 3 cycles. Ovulation ≈ 14 days before next period.
